# How to count calories and macros when your app doesn't know the food

Most calorie counters are built for other markets. You scan a local product and the app has never heard of it. Here's how to count calories without the hassle.

## Step 1. Work out your target
The most common way to estimate daily energy needs is the Mifflin–St Jeor equation. It uses sex, age, height, weight and activity level. To lose weight, a moderate deficit is usually subtracted; to gain, a surplus is added. Agree the exact numbers with a doctor or dietitian.

## Step 2. Log fast, or you'll quit
The main reason people stop counting calories is that it takes too long. What helps:
- **A barcode scanner** instead of searching by hand.
- **Recent foods**, because we eat roughly the same things every day.
- **Your own dishes:** build a recipe from ingredients once, then add it as one portion.

## Step 3. If a product isn't in the database
The nutrition table on the pack has everything you need: calories, protein, fat and carbs per 100 g. Enter it once and it's there next time.

## Step 4. Watch the trend, not one day
Weight moves around daily. Weekly and monthly summaries and the weight trend tell you much more.
