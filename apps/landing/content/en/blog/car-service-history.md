# How to keep a car service history (and stop losing receipts)

When did you last change the brake fluid? If the answer is "the year before last, I think", this article is for you.

## Why record anything at all
- **So you don't skip services.** Oil, filters and belts have intervals by mileage or time. A missed change usually costs more than the change itself.
- **To see what the car really costs.** The total of a year's receipts is often a surprise.
- **To sell for more.** Buyers trust a car with a full service history.
- **To avoid overpaying at the garage.** When you can see a part was replaced six months ago, it's easier to say no to "let's replace this too".

## What to record after every garage visit
1. Date and mileage.
2. What was done: work and parts (with part numbers if you have them).
3. What it cost.
4. A photo of the receipt or invoice.
5. When the next change is due, by date or mileage.

## Where to keep it
- **A paper service book:** handy at the garage, but it gets lost and never reminds you.
- **A spreadsheet or notes:** flexible, but hard to search and easy to abandon.
- **A dedicated app:** entries, receipts and reminders in one place.
